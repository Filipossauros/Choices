Section 01

Executive Summary

The entry into force of the EHDS Regulation (EU 2025/327) on 26 March 2025 marks a decisive turning point for EU digital health governance. The eHealth Network (eHN) Subgroups, which have operated on a voluntary cooperative model since their establishment, must now evolve to serve a fundamentally different institutional ecosystem.

This proposal argues that the transition to the EHDS Board does not diminish the need for MS-driven technical and semantic cooperation — it strengthens it. However, the current model has reached structural limits: declining engagement, unsustainable allocation of individuals as contributors, and a growing disconnection between subgroup activities and the formal regulatory process.

Under the provisions of Article 92 of the EHDS Regulation, the EHDS Board may work in subgroups for certain topics, in which digital health authorities shall be represented and which shall support the Board with specific expertise. We propose that reformed subgroups be established directly under the EHDS Board — with a clear advisory mandate, a revised expert composition, a structured relationship with SDOs, and funded resources that they can access. Critically, the subgroups must be repositioned not merely as regulatorily-constrained advisors but as the principal instrument through which Member States cooperate on EU digital health interoperability beyond the mandatory minimum.

Important caveat on remuneration: Article 7(1) of Implementing Regulation 2026/771 states that EHDS Board members participating in the activities of the EHDS Board shall not be remunerated by the Commission for their participation. This applies to Board members and, by extension, to SG participants acting in that capacity. Any funding model for SG activity must therefore operate through national authority budgets or separately scoped EC instruments.
Problem

Anticipation of the EHDS Regulation has led, to some extent, to EC and MS disengagement from SG activities. The voluntary model has reached structural limits. 75% of participants overlap across both SGs. No successor JA is planned to maintain or develop new technical assets and requirements — that development is also excluded from the Support Centre contract. A potential gap exists between formal adoption of EHDS implementing acts through comitology and the advisory role of the EHDS Board in securing MS political and technical alignment.

Opportunity

The transition period (2025–2027) offers a window to define the future SG model before it is formalised. SGs should act as providers of the technical input needed by both the EHDS Board and MS, enabling them to effectively represent positions within the comitology process. There is also an opportunity to avoid fragmenting SGs into wholly separate structures for primary and secondary data use.

Proposal

Reform the two SGs into two thematic groups directly under the EHDS Board. Establish a permanent EC-funded secretariat. Formalise SDO relationships. Allow access by the EHDS Board and, by extension, the SGs to the Support Centre services, creating bidirectional exchanges between them. Preserve the SGs as the MS-driven free-cooperation space for EU digital health interoperability.

Section 02

Context & Current Challenges

The eHN Subgroups were originally established with a broad mandate to support MS cooperation on digital health interoperability. The EHDS Regulation now formally structures this space but introduces significant governance uncertainties — most critically around who is responsible for developing and maintaining requirements and technical assets, and how technical MS knowledge might connect to formal regulatory outputs.

Current State (2026)

  • Two SGs (Technical Interoperability and Semantic) with approximately 75% participant overlap and convergence of scope. As of 2026, all mandates are effectively joint across both SGs.
  • Voluntary participation — no formal obligation or remuneration. EC and MS interest has declined as focus shifts to domestic EHDS compliance preparation.
  • Three taskforces retain genuine traction: Selection of Preferred Code Systems; Collaboration with EU Stakeholders and EU Projects; and Digital Testing Environment User Group.
  • Xt-EHR JA approaching closure (~mid-2026) — the primary producer of FHIR IGs, EEHRxF profiles, EHR system requirements, and conformance frameworks for primary use.
  • No successor JA announced. The Support Centre contract (HADEA/2024/OP/0014) is the EC's primary instrument — but explicitly excludes development of the solution itself and common specifications. The EC remains ultimately responsible for fulfilling these obligations.
  • MS interest in the Support Centre scope is high — but the mandate remains unclear in key respects.
  • The eHN Coordinated Actions meetings serve as a means for alignment but remain ultimately insufficient as a governance mechanism for the level of technical coordination required.

Key Structural Tensions

Comitology vs. Advisory Separation

The EC is responsible for producing EHDS technical assets. These are then regulated via implementing acts, approved through a comitology process. The EHDS Board has an advisory role — it may provide written contributions to inform EC and MS representatives in this comitology process, but does not make binding decisions. The SGs exist to ensure that this advisory process is grounded in genuine technical and operational MS expertise.

Asset Maintenance Gap

FHIR IGs, terminology mappings, testing assets and EEHRxF profiles produced by Xt-EHR require ongoing maintenance. The Support Centre contract explicitly excludes "the development of the solution itself and common specifications." No body has been formally assigned this responsibility post-Xt-EHR. The EC is ultimately responsible for fulfilling this obligation.

Disconnection from Implementing Acts

Current SG work is effectively disconnected from the production of EHDS implementing acts. This is a current major structural hurdle. The comitology process and the way MS are consulted within it have not involved the SGs in any meaningful way, resulting in a very poor initial draft of the EEHRxF implementing act. Reconnecting SG expertise to the implementing act drafting process is the single most important structural repair needed.

eHMSEG Legal Working Group — A Critical Input

The eHMSEG Legal Working Group (LWG) is undertaking legal revision work of particular relevance during this transition period. Its outputs — legal interpretations, positions on regulatory ambiguities, and assessments of implementing act adequacy — must be formally fed back into the SGs' advisory work. The SGs cannot produce technically coherent advisory positions without access to authoritative legal interpretation. This connection must be formalised.

Section 03

Governance Logic — How the Pieces Fit

Under the EHDS governance architecture there are three distinct but interrelated processes: the formal regulatory process (comitology), the political advisory process (EHDS Board), and the technical expert process (SGs). Each plays a different role, and their interconnections are shown below.

1 · Comitology — Formal Rule-Making

The European Commission is responsible for preparing and adopting the technical specifications and implementing acts underpinning the EHDS. These are adopted through a comitology procedure — the formal and legally binding mechanism for EU law implementation. SGs do not form part of this formal decision-making process.

2 · EHDS Board — Political Advisory

The EHDS Board exists to mitigate potential misalignment between measures adopted through comitology and the operational needs of Member States. The Board may provide written advisory contributions to inform both EC and MS representatives in comitology. The direction of advice is from the Board toward comitology — not the reverse.

3 · Subgroups — Technical Expert Foundation

The SGs provide the analytical depth required for the EHDS Board to exercise its advisory function credibly. Beyond supporting the Board, SG outputs also directly benefit MS. SGs operate directly under the EHDS Board pursuant to Art. 92(2) — not under the MyHealth@EU or HealthData@EU Steering Groups.

Section 05

The Support Centre — A Strategic Opportunity to Address Sustainability

The EHDS Support Centre (HADEA/2024/OP/0014) must be recognised not merely as a stakeholder-support instrument, but as the primary near-term opportunity to address the longstanding structural unsustainability of the SG model. Its relationship with the EHDS Board and the SGs must be bidirectional, formally governed, and adequately managed by the EC as contract manager.

The contractor engaged through this contract will progressively accumulate operational and technical knowledge throughout the contract duration — knowledge of the EEHRxF landscape, of implementer challenges, of specification gaps, and of the legal-technical interface. This accumulation of expertise makes the Support Centre uniquely positioned to support the development, governance, and maintenance of architectural principles and other advisory outputs in ways that the SGs alone cannot sustain on a purely voluntary basis.

Two-track approach: In the near term, the EC should direct adequate use of the Support Centre's contracted services to support the EHDS Board and SGs — this makes resources available immediately without requiring new procurement. In parallel, and only if required, a specific dedicated tender designed to directly support the EHDS Board and SGs should be considered for the medium term.

Bidirectional Relationship: Support Centre ↔ EHDS Board & SGs

Interactions between the Support Centre and the SGs must be genuinely bidirectional. The EHDS Board and its SGs should be able to make active use of the Support Centre's services, and the results of that engagement should enrich the contractor's knowledge base and tool ecosystem. The EC, as contract manager, is responsible for governing the adequate allocation of Support Centre capacity to these tasks.

The exclusion of "the development of the solution and common specifications" remains the most critical gap in EHDS primary use governance. The EC must assign this responsibility explicitly. The EHDS Board, informed by the SGs, should advise on the scope of the strategic instrument needed to fill it.
SGs and the CoP are distinct: The Community of Practice is an implementer-facing support instrument for EHR system manufacturers and healthcare providers. The SGs are a governance advisory body composed of digital health authority representatives. SG members may individually participate in CoP activities, but the SGs as bodies have no governance relationship with the CoP.

Disconnection from Implementing Acts: A Structural Failure to Repair

Current SG work is effectively disconnected from the production of EHDS implementing acts. This is not incidental — it is a structural consequence of the comitology process and the way MS are consulted within it. SG expert positions have not been meaningfully incorporated into the implementing acts drafting, and the result has been a very poor initial draft of the EEHRxF implementing act.

The Support Centre's monitoring and feedback outputs, combined with formal SG advisory positions, should be embedded in the implementing acts revision and development cycle. The EHDS Board's written contributions to the comitology process must be grounded in SG expert analysis. Without this connection, the pattern will repeat in every subsequent implementing act.

Section 06

SDO Relationships — Strategic Partnerships for the EEHRxF

Standards Development Organisations play a strategic and indispensable role in the EHDS technical ecosystem. However, the responsibility for producing EHDS requirements and technical assets must not be delegated from the European Commission to SDOs. The EHDS Board, supported by the SGs, must play a central role in overseeing the development of these assets and in advising on the strategic tenders that shape them.

SDOs should support the development of requirements and technical assets — providing subject matter expertise, standards frameworks, and international alignment — but accountability for those assets remains with the EC. Legal agreements or contracts between the EC and SDOs are necessary, but must ensure SDO independence is preserved. The EHDS Board and SGs should be part of the arrangements governing those relationships. Potential strategic tenders designed to shape the assets and requirements flowing from the EHDS Regulation's obligations should be foreseen, taking into account advisory positions from the SGs.

Strategic Role of Individual SDOs

HL7 Europe → SG-1

Xt-EHR worked directly with HL7 Europe — not HL7 International — to produce FHIR IGs for the EEHRxF. This relationship should be formalised post-Xt-EHR through a legal arrangement between the EC and HL7 Europe. SG-1 should have a standing liaison role in that arrangement. FHIR IG ballot processes must be coordinated with the SG advisory cycle so that MS positions are coherent and SG expertise shapes the FHIR profiles adopted as EHDS assets.

SNOMED International → SG-1

SNOMED International is among the better-organised SDOs in this ecosystem, making structured engagement more tractable. National Release Centres participate through MLDS. SG-1 should formalise coordination for EHDS-specific extension guidance and national adaptation alignment. A positive example of concrete SDO cooperation is the SNOMED International Entity Linking Challenge — a model for how SDOs can engage subject matter experts and produce quality technical outputs that feed directly into advisory work. Licensing arrangements for EHDS use cases must be addressed through EC-SDO legal agreements.

IHE Europe → SG-1 / TF Testing

IHE Europe uses IHE International profiles — it does not develop them independently. Conformance testing (Connectathon, Gazelle) is critical for EHDS compliance. The Digital Testing Environment User Group taskforce is the primary liaison. Alignment between EHDS compliance milestones and the IHE testing event calendar is operationally essential and must be formalised.

Section 08

Key Recommendations

The following recommendations are addressed to the European Commission, to Member State representatives, and to the eHN community, ordered by urgency.

1

Direct proportional Support Centre capacity to service the EHDS Board and SGs — immediately

The EC, as contract manager of the Support Centre, should direct a defined proportion of contracted capacity to service the EHDS Board and SGs — providing resources for knowledge aggregation, architectural principle development, legal interpretation channelling (via eHMSEG LWG outputs), and advisory output facilitation. This is the fastest available mechanism to address SG sustainability without new procurement. Interactions between the SGs and the Support Centre must be bidirectional: SG expert positions feed into the knowledge base and tools; Support Centre monitoring and intelligence feed into SG advisory work.

Urgent — Q3 2026EC Action (contract management)
2

Formally identify which body will develop new FHIR IGs and common specifications post-Xt-EHR, and initiate a strategic tender

The Support Centre explicitly excludes "the development of the solution itself and common specifications." The EC must, by Q3 2026, identify the body responsible for this work. The EHDS Board should advise on the scope of a strategic tender for EHDS asset and requirement development — this is precisely the kind of strategic advisory contribution the Board is designed to make. The SGs should provide the expert input grounding that tender scope. SDOs can contribute to delivery but accountability remains with the EC.

Urgent — Q3 2026EC Action · EHDS Board advisory
3

Initiate a dedicated tender to directly support the EHDS Board and SGs

In parallel with using the Support Centre's capacity, a specific dedicated procurement should be initiated — designed to address the structural shortcomings of the voluntary SG model and to provide the EHDS Board and SGs with dedicated technical and secretariat support, a permanent advisory knowledge base, and the operational infrastructure needed for sustainable functioning. The EHDS Board should advise the EC on the scope of this tender. This is the medium-term path to genuine sustainability beyond what contract management of the current Support Centre can achieve.

2026–2027EC Action · EHDS Board advisory
4

Reconnect the SGs to the implementing act drafting process — and address the EEHRxF IA quality gap

The disconnection of SG expert work from the production of EHDS implementing acts is a structural failure that has already produced a poor initial draft of the EEHRxF implementing act. The reformed SGs must be formally integrated into the IA revision and development cycle: SG advisory positions must be produced on a timeline aligned with the comitology process, submitted to the EHDS Board for formal advisory contribution, and reflected in IA drafts. The eHMSEG Legal Working Group's outputs must feed into this process — both through the SGs and through the Support Centre knowledge base.

Urgent — before 2027EC + SG Action
5

Reform the two existing SGs into two thematic groups directly under the EHDS Board

Two reformed thematic groups should be established under Art. 92(2): SG-1 (Semantic and Syntactic Interoperability) absorbing the current effective joint mandate, and SG-2 (Architecture and Cross-cutting) addressing the broader cross-regulatory and emergent domains agenda — including AI, AI-enabled system trust and quality, trust guidelines, and cross-regulatory alignment (AI Act, eIDAS, NIS2). These groups are not under the MyHealth@EU or HealthData@EU Steering Groups. MS must revise expert nominations to bring domain-specific profiles.

2026–2027MS + EC Action
6

Establish legal agreements between the EC and SDOs, with EHDS Board and SG involvement in those arrangements

Legal agreements or contracts between the EC and SDOs — HL7 Europe, SNOMED International, and IHE Europe — are necessary to formalise the standards development relationship. These must preserve SDO independence. The EHDS Board and SGs must be part of the governance of those arrangements. SG-1 in particular should work with SDOs to access subject matter experts, with the SNOMED International Entity Linking Challenge as a model for productive SDO cooperation. All assets produced under EC-SDO arrangements must be published under open licences.

2026–2028EC Action · EHDS Board + SG advisory
7

Enshrine SG mandate, bidirectional SC relationship, and SDO governance role in the EHDS Board rules of procedure

The rules of procedure must explicitly define: (a) SG mandate including advisory input to implementing acts, bidirectional engagement with the Support Centre, cooperation with SDOs, and MS free-cooperation space; (b) the Support Centre reporting and service-use obligations toward the SGs; (c) the EHDS Board's role in advising on strategic tenders and SDO legal arrangements; (d) integration of eHMSEG LWG legal interpretation outputs into SG advisory work; (e) non-relationships between SGs and the CoP, Stakeholder Forum, and Art. 95 Steering Groups.

2026–2027Strategic
8

Replace — not supplement — the eHN Coordinated Actions alignment function

The Coordinated Actions meetings are insufficient as a governance mechanism — without formal authority, without connection to the comitology timeline, and without structured advisory outputs. The reformed SGs must replace this function. Coordinated Actions may continue for operational matters, but must not be positioned as a governance substitute.

2026–2027EC + MS Action
9

Conduct a formal SG review at the 2029 EHDS application milestone

At EHDS entry into application (March 2029), formally review: mandate relevance, composition adequacy, output quality, SDO coordination effectiveness, funding sufficiency, and adequacy of the Support Centre bidirectional relationship. Results feed into the operating model for 2029–2031, including whether permanent budget structures are warranted.

⚑

The Central Strategic Question

What is the reason for the existence of the Subgroups under the EHDS Board? The answer proposed here is twofold. First, they are the technical expert foundation that gives the EHDS Board's advisory function credibility — without them, the Board cannot produce the quality of written contributions needed to genuinely align comitology decisions with MS needs. Second, they are the instrument through which Member States cooperate on EU digital health interoperability beyond the regulatory minimum — the free-cooperation space that no other structure in the EHDS ecosystem provides. Both functions are essential. Neither can be delivered by the Support Centre alone, the Coordinated Actions meetings, or the Stakeholder Forum. But the Support Centre, properly engaged through bidirectional cooperation and proportional EC-managed use, is the single most immediate opportunity to make those functions sustainable. Only a reformed, well-resourced, and properly mandated SG structure — backed by a Support Centre relationship, SDO partnerships, and a clear connection to the comitology process — can deliver on the promise of the EHDS.
